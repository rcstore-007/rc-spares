Problems

Speed controller blinking or car won't move

The ESC flashes or beeps, the motor stops, or the car has no power at all.

Why it happens

  • The battery is flat, or the low-voltage cutoff has switched on.
  • The ESC (electronic speed controller) or motor got too hot and paused to protect itself.
  • The ESC isn't calibrated to your controller, or the throttle trim isn't at zero.
  • A loose motor or battery wire.

Check these, in order

  1. Most blinking is a safety message, not damage. Look up the blink pattern in your ESC's manual first.
  2. Charge the battery and check the ESC is set to the right battery type.
  3. If the motor or ESC is very hot, let them cool by themselves. Don't put them in a fridge or freezer.
  4. Set the throttle trim knob on the controller back to zero.
  5. Calibrate the ESC again following its manual.
  6. Check the three motor wires and the battery plug are pushed in fully and not burnt.

Careful: If it keeps overheating, fit a smaller pinion and check nothing is dragging. Running hot again and again will damage the motor and ESC.

Good to know: A program card lets you change ESC settings like cutoff voltage and brake strength without counting beeps.

Common questions

Why is my ESC blinking red?

On most ESCs a blinking red light means low voltage or overheating. Charge the battery, let everything cool, and check the manual for that exact pattern.

Why is my ESC blinking red and green?

Commonly a battery-type mismatch or voltage protection: for example LiPo cutoff selected with a NiMH pack, or too many cells. Check the battery setting and your ESC's maximum voltage.

How do I calibrate my ESC?

Most ESCs: hold the set button while switching on, then show it neutral, full throttle and full brake as the beeps or lights prompt. Follow your ESC's manual for the exact sequence.
